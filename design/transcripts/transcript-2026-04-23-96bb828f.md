**User:** They can run queries and create algorithms to analyze their data allowing them to evaluate their decisions and project the future.

It would also allow them to determine outcomes they’ve experienced.

These query/algorithms will be available to trade at the market

**Assistant:** This is a genuinely novel model. Capturing what I'm hearing:

**Personal analytics engine:**
- Users write or configure queries and algorithms against their own data
- Retrospective: evaluate past decisions, trace outcomes they've experienced
- Prospective: project likely futures based on patterns in their own data
- All computation runs on their data, inside their zero-knowledge boundary

**The algorithm marketplace:**
- Users can publish and trade the algorithms themselves — not their data
- Someone builds a great "track decision fatigue" or "predict savings rate" algorithm → lists it on the market
- Buyers run it against their own private data — the algorithm travels, the data never does
- Creates a real economy around personal insight tooling

**This is the key architectural insight:**
- Data stays sovereign and encrypted with the user
- Algorithms are the tradeable, shareable asset
- The marketplace is the network effect and the business model

This also has deep implications for the data hub — it needs to be a proper **local query engine** (think: SQLite or DuckDB running on the user's device) capable of executing user-defined or marketplace-sourced algorithms against local data without that data ever leaving the device.

Keep going — I'm especially curious whether you're thinking about what *kinds* of data the suite collects beyond what the apps already cover (financial, health, habits, etc.).
