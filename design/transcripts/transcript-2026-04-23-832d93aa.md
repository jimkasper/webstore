**Assistant:** Yes, this model works — and it's a smart monetization structure. But there's one category of risk worth flagging before you continue:

**Cross-tier interaction leakage**

The hard problem isn't protecting Tier 1 users from their own data — that's straightforward. The risk is what lower-tier users' data reveals *about* Tier 1 users:

- A Tier 5 user's social graph shows they're connected to *someone* — that "someone" could be identifiable even if we never touch Tier 1's data
- A Tier 5 user's message metadata (timestamps, frequency) can reveal patterns about their Tier 1 contact
- If a Tier 5 user is in a group with Tier 1 users, group membership data collected from Tier 5 could expose Tier 1 identities

**This is solvable, but it requires deliberate design:**
- When collecting data from lower-tier users, any reference to a higher-tier user must be anonymized or dropped before storage
- Higher-tier users may need the option to limit visibility to lower-tier users entirely
- Interaction metadata between tiers needs scrubbing rules

**Upgrading (e.g. Tier 5 → Tier 1)**

**User:** Explain the options

**Assistant:** **1. On upgrade — what happens to previously collected data?**

**Delete it**
- User's past data is permanently purged from our systems
- Strongest privacy guarantee, builds trust, aligns with "you control your data"
- Con: we lose potentially valuable anonymized/aggregate insights we already had

**Anonymize and retain**
- Strip all identifying info, keep the behavioral patterns in aggregate
- We retain some analytical value, user loses nothing sensitive
- Con: true anonymization is hard — re-identification attacks are a real risk, especially for unique behavioral patterns

**Freeze and retain**
- Keep it as-is but stop collecting new data under old rules
- User can request deletion separately
- Con: weakest option from the user's perspective — we still hold their identified data

---

**2. On downgrade — what happens to higher-tier connections?**

**Silently gate**
- The connection is quietly suspended — neither user can interact until resolved
- The higher-tier user sees a generic "this contact no longer meets your privacy settings" notice, no tier revealed
- Con: confusing without context, could damage relationships without explanation

**Notify with detail**
- Higher-tier user is told exactly what tier their contact moved to
- More transparent, lets them make an informed decision
- Con: exposes the downgraded user's tier — which is their private financial/lifestyle info

**Notify without detail**
- Higher-tier user is told "a contact changed tiers and no longer meets your settings" — no tier revealed
- Balances transparency with the downgraded user's privacy
- They then choose: keep the connection anyway, suspend it, or remove it

---

**3. Grace period before new data rules kick in?**

**No grace period**
- Rules change immediately at tier switch
- Cleanest, most predictable, aligns with what the user signed up for
- Con: no buffer if someone accidentally downgrades or is mid-billing cycle

**Short grace period (e.g. 7–14 days)**
- New tier is active for billing/features immediately, but data collection rules transition over a short window
- Gives users time to realize a mistake and revert without exposure
- Con: creates a brief inconsistency between what tier they're on and what data we collect

**Billing cycle grace**
- Data rules change at the end of the current billing cycle, not immediately
- Feels fair — they paid for the current period
- Con: longest window of inconsistency, more complex to implement
